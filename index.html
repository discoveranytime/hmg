01Two in one

What HMG actually is

HMG — Human Menopausal Gonadotropin — is a fertility drug carrying two gonadotropins in one vial: FSH and LH. Its unusual origin: it’s purified from the urine of postmenopausal women, whose bodies naturally excrete high levels of both hormones. Approved, prescription, and a workhorse of IVF.

Schematic — not to scale. FSH and LH are large glycoproteins; the 1:1 activity ratio is the point.
⚠️ Terminology matters: HMG is a biological gonadotropin preparation, not a synthetic short peptide. FSH and LH are large glycoprotein heterodimers (two protein subunits plus sugar chains) — calling HMG “a peptide” misrepresents both its molecular structure and how it works.
FSH and LH are ~200-amino-acid glycoprotein heterodimers — roughly 20× the size of a typical research peptide. The sugar chains matter for how they behave in the body.

Think of it this way: each FSH or LH molecule is like a two-piece building block wearing a sugar coat. The α piece is identical in both hormones — the β piece is the name tag that decides whether it acts as FSH or LH. And that sugar coating isn’t decoration: it helps set how long the hormone survives in the blood.

Biological preparation, not a uniform molecule

HMG is a purified biological extract, not one chemically uniform compound. Isoform mix and exact FSH:LH activity can vary between products and batches — product composition and manufacturing quality matter, and this is one reason highly purified and recombinant alternatives exist.

Think of it this way: orange juice labeled “not from concentrate” tastes slightly different from carton to carton — it’s a natural extract, not a formula. HMG is closer to that carton than to a soda mixed from an exact recipe, which is why product composition and manufacturing quality matter so much.

Two hormones, one vial

HMG delivers FSH plus LH activity together — FSH grows the follicles, LH helps them mature. One product covering both halves of the ovulation equation.

Urine-derived — really

Postmenopausal urine is rich in gonadotropins. Donors collect it, labs purify it — one of medicine’s strangest supply chains, running for over half a century.

IVF workhorse

An approved prescription fertility drug (best known as Menopur): ovulation induction and controlled ovarian stimulation for IVF, used under close specialist monitoring.

Plain English

Think of follicle-growing as a two-person job: FSH is the builder framing the house, LH is the electrician wiring it. HMG is the contractor who brings both crews in one van — sourced, improbably, from a donation program you’d never guess.

02The collection

The strangest supply chain in medicine

Before recombinant DNA, there was no way to manufacture human hormones — so medicine went collecting. The HMG story is resourcefulness at its most literal.

Half a century of turning a waste product into babies.
The insight

After menopause, the pituitary pumps out gonadotropins trying to wake unresponsive ovaries — and the excess leaves in urine. Researchers realized this waste stream was a hormone mine.

The collection

Donation programs gathered urine from postmenopausal volunteers; labs extracted and purified the FSH and LH. Crude by modern standards — and the first effective fertility drugs in history.

Think of it this way: after menopause, the pituitary shouts louder and louder at ovaries that can no longer hear it — and the excess signal washes out in urine. HMG’s supply chain simply collects that washout, turning a biological shout into a medicine.

Refined, not replaced

Purification improved for decades (today’s Menopur is highly standardized). Recombinant FSH arrived in 1995 — lab-made, pure — yet HMG never left: many specialists still prefer the FSH+LH combo.

Plain English

It’s the ultimate trash-to-treasure story: a bodily waste product, collected in bins, refined in labs, turned into millions of pregnancies. Biotech eventually built a cleaner factory — but the original mine never closed.

03How it works

How it’s supposed to work

No “supposed” needed — this is decades-proven reproductive medicine. HMG simply replaces the pituitary’s signal with a stronger, controllable one.

Endogenous order: GnRH pulses → pituitary FSH/LH → gonadal receptors. HMG enters only at the FSH/LH step — it is not GnRH and not a pituitary signal.

Think of it this way: the brain doesn’t lean on the pituitary’s doorbell — it rings it in short bursts, because a constant ring would make the pituitary stop answering. The whole axis is a chain of command: brain → pituitary → ovaries. HMG skips the first two links and delivers the order straight to the ovaries.

Signaling → recruitment → maturation → downstream events. Every step monitored with ultrasounds and bloodwork.

Think of it this way: in a natural month, one follicle wins the race. HMG is like a coach inviting several runners onto the track at once — which is why one IVF cycle can yield multiple eggs, and why every step needs ultrasound monitoring.

FSH acts on Sertoli cells inside the tubule; LH acts on Leydig cells outside it. Two different pathways, one shared result — actual tubule anatomy, not a generic "testosterone up" graphic.

Think of it this way: Sertoli cells are the nursery staff inside the tubule, feeding and nursing the developing sperm. Leydig cells are a separate factory outside the tubule, making testosterone. FSH gives its orders to the nursery; LH gives its orders to the factory.

✅ What HMG does

  • Provides FSH + LH activity at gonadal receptors
  • Supports follicular recruitment and growth in a monitored cycle
  • Supports spermatogenesis in appropriate male protocols
  • Works through established endocrine signaling, specialist-led

❌ What it doesn’t guarantee

  • Does not automatically produce ovulation in every patient
  • Does not guarantee fertility, pregnancy, or testosterone normalization
  • Is not a shortcut, enhancer, or general vitality product
  • Does not work outside the clinical protocol that delivers it

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

Sixty-plus years of clinical use and millions of IVF cycles. The evidence question isn’t whether HMG works — it’s when it beats the recombinant alternative.

It works — enormously

HMG has driven countless ovulations and IVF pregnancies since the 1960s. The efficacy file is one of the deepest in reproductive medicine.

HMG vs. recombinant: a draw-ish

Head-to-head trials vs. recombinant FSH show broadly similar live-birth rates. Subgroup debates (older patients, LH benefit) keep both products alive — and keep conferences lively.

The monitoring is the medicine

Outcomes depend as much on protocol and monitoring as on the vial: dosing adjustments, trigger timing, OHSS prevention. The drug is one instrument in an orchestra.

HMG's established medical context is one of the deepest in reproductive medicine. Claims circulating in peptide communities belong to a different evidence world entirely.

Think of it this way: a sixty-year medical file and a forum thread are both “information” — the way a building-inspection report and a restaurant review are both “opinions.” Same word, completely different weight.

Plain English

HMG’s evidence file is a library, not a folder: six decades, millions of cycles. The interesting argument was never “does it work” — it’s the civil war with recombinant FSH, fought in subgroup analyses at fertility conferences, with live-birth rates as the scoreboard.

05Axis players

HMG vs the whole axis

Five names, five jobs in the reproductive axis: GnRH, FSH, LH, hCG, and HMG. They are not interchangeable — each acts through different receptors at a different point in the signaling chain. Here’s who sits where.

HMG · the donation

Urine-derived: FSH + LH activity together, decades of history, lower cost in many markets. The combo theory: LH helps certain patients.

Recombinant FSH · the clean room

Lab-made (e.g., follitropin): pure FSH, no LH, batch-to-batch consistency, no donation chain. The purity theory: precise, predictable, modern.

The verdict: it depends

Trials show similar overall success. Specialists pick by patient: HMG’s LH for some, recombinant’s precision for others — personalized, not ideological.

HMG ≠ GnRH agonist

A GnRH agonist works through the pituitary — modulating the gland’s own output. HMG bypasses the pituitary completely, delivering gonadotropin activity straight to the gonads. Different floor of the building.

HMG ≠ hCG

hCG acts on the same receptor as LH and mimics LH — but carries no FSH activity. HMG brings both FSH and LH to the table; confusing the two drops half the mechanism.

Think of it this way: hCG is an understudy who only knows half the role — it copies LH but can’t do FSH’s job. HMG is the full double act, FSH and LH together. Confuse the two and you lose half the play.

Plain English

It’s farmers’ market vs. laboratory: one is a natural extract with character (and LH), the other is pharmaceutical-grade pure. Patients mostly care about one number — live births — and on that scoreboard, the 1960s donation-bin drug still ties the 1990s biotech marvel.

06Potential benefits

Potential benefits

HMG is established fertility medicine — so unlike most compounds on this site, its benefit story starts on solid ground. Still read it in tiers: what trials proved, what is still debated, and what forums say. The tiers are not equal, and they are not meant to be read as equal.

Tier 1 · Established

What six decades of medicine established

Approved indications and outcomes measured in randomized trials and millions of cycles.

  • Ovulation induction. For women who don't ovulate on their own, HMG reliably produces ovulation — its original and enduring job.
  • Controlled ovarian stimulation for IVF. Grows multiple follicles per cycle — the engine of modern IVF since the 1960s.
  • Live births on par with recombinant FSH. Head-to-head trials show broadly similar live-birth rates — the benefit is real whichever manufacturing philosophy you pick.
  • Male gonadotropin therapy. In specialist care, gonadotropin regimens including HMG-type activity support spermatogenesis in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism — established, but strictly specialist territory.

Think of it this way: this tier is the deeds registry — sixty years of recorded transactions, audited and public. You don't have to take anyone's word for HMG's benefits; the paperwork is filed.

Tier 2 · Predicted / Emerging

What the field is still working out

Real research questions — explicitly predicted and emerging, never settled.

  • The LH benefit question. Predicted: HMG's LH component may help specific subgroups — older patients, poor responders — argued with data, still unresolved.
  • Personalized protocols. Emerging: matching HMG vs. recombinant to biomarkers and patient profiles rather than habit — the field's active research frontier.
  • Long-term reassurance. Large studies on repeated stimulation are reassuring but not definitive — the evidence file keeps growing.

Think of it this way: tier 2 is the renovation in progress — the house is solid and lived-in; the crew is still arguing about which rooms get the upgrade.

Tier 3 · Community

What IVF forums talk about

Unverified anecdotes — paraphrased recurring themes from forums and social media: not verified, not evidence.

  • Stim diaries. Precise, quantified cycle logs — follicle counts, estradiol numbers — invaluable for solidarity, still n=1 stories.
  • "Switched and got more eggs." Brand-switching success stories — but cycle-to-cycle variation confounds every one of them.
  • The bloat and the stings. Side-effect commiseration — real-feeling, widely shared, and no substitute for monitoring.

Think of it this way: the forums are the waiting room — full of people who've been through it, generous with comfort and tips. Waiting rooms heal spirits; they don't run trials.

07Who it may suit

Who it may suit — and who it should not

HMG has established medical indications — which means this chapter can name real patient populations, not just research settings. Still general information, not a personal recommendation: fertility treatment is individual, protocol-driven, and specialist-supervised.

May be relevant for

Populations with established indications — always under specialist care.

  • Women with ovulatory disorders — HMG is an established option for ovulation induction when first-line approaches aren't suitable, under a reproductive endocrinologist.
  • IVF patients — controlled ovarian stimulation is HMG's heaviest-established use: growing multiple follicles for retrieval.
  • Men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism — gonadotropin therapy to support spermatogenesis, strictly in specialist care.

May not suit / extra caution

Situations where HMG is inappropriate or demands specialist judgment.

  • Anyone outside specialist care — ovarian stimulation without ultrasound and hormone monitoring risks OHSS; this is never a DIY drug.
  • People who are already pregnant — gonadotropins have no role in maintaining pregnancy; stimulation is a pre-conception therapy.
  • Hormone-dependent tumors or undiagnosed abnormal bleeding — require full workup first; stimulation is contraindicated until cleared.
  • Uncontrolled thyroid or adrenal disorders — these must be corrected before any fertility treatment begins.
  • Those expecting a guaranteed outcome — even established medicine offers per-cycle odds, not promises.
This is general information, not personal medical advice. HMG is a prescription fertility medicine with real indications and real risks — nothing here recommends for or against it. Fertility decisions belong with a licensed clinician, ideally a reproductive endocrinologist, who knows your individual situation.

09Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

A powerful, well-studied drug with serious, well-known risks — managed by monitoring, not by luck.

OHSS

Known risk: ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome — swollen ovaries, fluid shifts, in severe cases hospitalization. The reason dosing is titrated by specialists with ultrasound monitoring.

Multiple pregnancy

Known risk: stimulating multiple follicles raises twin/triplet rates — with all their maternal and fetal complications. Single-embryo transfer policies exist largely because of this.

Injection burden

Known: weeks of daily injections, bloating, mood swings from the hormonal rollercoaster. The physical and emotional toll is the part trials under-describe.

Long-term ovary questions

Debated: whether repeated stimulation affects long-term ovarian reserve or cancer risk. Large studies are reassuring but not definitive — a genuinely open file.

Optimal LH use

Unknown: exactly which patients benefit from HMG’s LH component vs. pure FSH. The field’s central unanswered question — argued with data, still unresolved.

Gray-market misuse

Unknown: unregulated “HMG” plus DIY protocols is among the most dangerous gray-market scenarios — OHSS without monitoring can kill.

Plain English

Known: OHSS, multiples, and the injection gauntlet — serious, managed, disclosed. Debated: long-term ovarian effects. Unknown: the LH question and what DIY users risk. This is what a mature risk file looks like: honest, quantified, and still worth reading carefully.

10Understanding HMG terminology

Understanding HMG (Human Menopausal Gonadotropin) terminology

Twelve terms, each with the science and a plain-English translation. Tap any term to expand it.

The scienceHuman menopausal gonadotropin (menotropin) is a purified gonadotropin preparation derived from the urine of postmenopausal women. It contains two biologically active hormones — follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) activity — reflecting the elevated gonadotropin output that follows menopause. Both FSH and LH are glycoprotein hormones built as heterodimers: two different protein chains joined together, sharing a common alpha subunit alongside a hormone-specific beta subunit. HMG preparations are standardized by total gonadotropin activity and used in fertility medicine to stimulate ovarian follicle development. It was the first major class of modern fertility drug, introduced in the 1960s, and modern versions such as Menopur are highly purified forms of the same preparation.

Plain EnglishHMG is a fertility drug that delivers two natural hormones — FSH and LH — in a single preparation. It originally came from a surprising source: the urine of women who had gone through menopause, because their bodies naturally produce high levels of these hormones. Each hormone is made of two protein pieces joined together, with one piece shared between them. Doctors use it to nudge the ovaries into developing follicles, which is the first step in fertility treatments like IVF. Modern versions are heavily purified, but it is the same drug class that opened the door to modern fertility medicine in the 1960s.

The scienceFollicle-stimulating hormone is a gonadotropin secreted by the anterior pituitary gland, whose release is driven by pulsatile GnRH signals from the hypothalamus. In women, FSH acts on granulosa cells of ovarian follicles and drives the recruitment and growth of those follicles in the first half of the menstrual cycle. It is a glycoprotein heterodimer composed of a common alpha subunit and a hormone-specific beta subunit. FSH activity is one of the two active components of HMG preparations, and recombinant forms such as follitropin alfa are manufactured by engineered cells. In men, FSH acts on Sertoli cells in the testes to support sperm production.

Plain EnglishFSH is one of the pituitary gland's two main messengers to the reproductive organs. In women, it tells the follicles — the tiny sacs in the ovaries that hold eggs — to start growing at the beginning of the menstrual cycle. A small region in the brain, the hypothalamus, fires off rhythmic pulses that prompt the pituitary to release FSH. It is one of the two active ingredients in HMG, and lab-made versions are also used in fertility treatment. In men, the same hormone helps sperm production along.

The scienceLuteinizing hormone is a gonadotropin secreted by the anterior pituitary gland in response to pulsatile GnRH signals from the hypothalamus. In women, a mid-cycle surge of LH triggers the final maturation of the dominant follicle and ovulation, and afterward LH supports the corpus luteum's production of progesterone. It is a glycoprotein heterodimer built from a common alpha subunit and an LH-specific beta subunit. LH activity is the second active component of HMG preparations. In men, LH stimulates the Leydig cells of the testes to produce testosterone.

Plain EnglishLH is the pituitary's other big reproductive messenger, working alongside FSH. In women, a sudden surge of LH in the middle of the cycle is what makes the mature egg release from its follicle — that is ovulation. Afterward, LH keeps the leftover follicle, the corpus luteum, going so it can make progesterone, which prepares the uterus for a possible pregnancy. It is the second active ingredient in HMG. In men, LH tells the testes to produce testosterone.

The scienceMenopur is a brand name (Ferring Pharmaceuticals) for a highly purified preparation of menotropins — the FSH and LH activity of human menopausal gonadotropin. It is manufactured from the same urinary-derived starting material as classic HMG, but subjected to extensive purification and standardized by total gonadotropin activity. Its active components are the same glycoprotein heterodimers, FSH and LH, found in all HMG preparations. Menopur is used in fertility medicine for ovarian stimulation in IVF and for ovulation induction, under medical supervision. It represents the modern, quality-controlled descendant of the original 1960s urinary extracts.

Plain EnglishMenopur is a brand-name version of HMG, made by the company Ferring. It comes from the same urinary-derived source as the original drug, but goes through heavy purification and quality testing so every batch delivers a consistent, standardized amount of FSH and LH activity. Its active ingredients are the same two hormones — FSH and LH — that HMG has always contained. Doctors use it for ovarian stimulation during IVF and for helping women ovulate. Think of it as the modern, quality-controlled descendant of the 1960s original.

The scienceIn vitro fertilization is an assisted reproductive technology in which mature eggs are retrieved from the ovaries and fertilized by sperm outside the body, in a laboratory. Ovarian stimulation with gonadotropins such as HMG is typically the first step, used to mature multiple follicles so that several eggs can be collected in one cycle. After fertilization, the resulting embryos are cultured for several days, and one or more are then transferred into the uterus. Any remaining viable embryos may be frozen for later use. IVF is used for a range of infertility causes, including blocked fallopian tubes, male-factor infertility, and unexplained infertility.

Plain EnglishIVF is a fertility treatment where eggs are collected from the ovaries and combined with sperm in a lab — fertilization happens outside the body. The cycle usually starts with hormone stimulation, which is where drugs like HMG come in, so that several eggs mature at once instead of just one. The fertilized eggs grow into embryos over a few days, and then one or more are placed into the uterus. Extra healthy embryos can be frozen for future attempts. It is used when couples face problems like blocked tubes, low sperm counts, or infertility with no clear cause.

The scienceOvulation induction is the medical use of fertility drugs to stimulate the development and release of one or more eggs from the ovaries. It is primarily used in women with anovulation — menstrual cycles in which no egg is released — such as in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Gonadotropins such as HMG provide direct FSH and LH stimulation to the ovaries, stepping in where the body's own signaling is deficient. Treatment requires close ultrasound and hormone monitoring so the clinician can time the cycle and watch for an excessive response. It is distinct from controlled ovarian hyperstimulation for IVF, where the goal is many eggs rather than one or two.

Plain EnglishOvulation induction means using fertility drugs to help the ovaries release an egg when they are not doing it on their own. It is mainly for women whose cycles skip ovulation — a common issue in conditions like PCOS. Drugs like HMG deliver the FSH and LH signals directly, stepping in where the body's own signals are falling short. Doctors monitor each cycle closely with ultrasounds and blood tests to time things right and keep the response safe. Unlike IVF stimulation, which aims for many eggs, ovulation induction usually aims for just one or two.

The scienceOvarian hyperstimulation syndrome is a potentially serious complication of ovarian stimulation with gonadotropins, in which the ovaries become enlarged and blood vessels become abnormally permeable. Fluid shifts from the bloodstream into the abdomen and sometimes the chest, causing abdominal swelling and discomfort. Most cases are mild and resolve on their own, but severe OHSS can involve significant fluid accumulation, blood concentration, and, rarely, blood clots or kidney problems. Risk is higher in women who develop very many follicles during stimulation, particularly younger women with polycystic ovaries. Careful monitoring and conservative stimulation strategies are used to reduce its likelihood.

Plain EnglishOHSS is the most feared side effect of the drugs used to stimulate the ovaries. When the ovaries over-respond, they swell and their blood vessels start leaking fluid into the belly. Most cases are mild — some bloating and discomfort that passes on its own — but severe cases can cause serious fluid buildup and other complications. The risk is higher when stimulation produces a very large number of follicles. Clinics try to prevent it through careful monitoring and conservative treatment plans.

The scienceRecombinant FSH is follicle-stimulating hormone manufactured by genetically engineered cells rather than extracted from urine. Examples include follitropin alfa and follitropin beta. Because it is produced in a controlled biotech process, it contains pure FSH with no LH activity and no urinary proteins. It is used for the same fertility indications as HMG — ovarian stimulation for IVF and ovulation induction — but delivers FSH alone. The long-running clinical debate between urinary HMG and recombinant FSH centers on whether the added LH activity of HMG changes outcomes such as live-birth rates.

Plain EnglishRecombinant FSH is lab-made FSH — built by engineered cells in a biotech facility instead of being extracted from urine. Common versions include follitropin alfa and follitropin beta. The manufacturing process is so controlled that the product is pure FSH: no LH activity and no leftover urinary proteins. It is used for the same fertility treatments as HMG, but it supplies FSH alone. The ongoing debate in fertility medicine is whether HMG's extra LH activity gives it any edge — and doctors compare the two by looking at live-birth rates.

The scienceThe trigger shot is the final hormone dose in an ovarian stimulation cycle, given to complete the maturation of the eggs and to time ovulation or egg retrieval precisely. It is usually human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) or a GnRH agonist, both of which mimic the natural mid-cycle LH surge. This surge-like signal prompts the mature follicles to finish egg development and be ready for retrieval about 36 hours later in an IVF cycle. Without this step, the follicles would not reach the final stage of readiness. The trigger is always given under medical supervision as part of a monitored stimulation protocol.

Plain EnglishThe trigger shot is the final step of an ovarian stimulation cycle — it finishes maturing the eggs and sets the exact timing for ovulation or egg collection. It is usually hCG or a GnRH agonist, and both act as a stand-in for the body's natural LH surge. This artificial surge tells the mature follicles to finish developing the eggs now. In IVF, the clinic then schedules egg retrieval for about a day and a half later. Without the trigger, the follicles would never reach full readiness.

The scienceAn ovarian follicle is the fluid-filled sac within the ovary that contains and nurtures a single immature egg, or oocyte. Each follicle is lined with granulosa cells, which respond to FSH and support the egg's growth and hormone production. During a natural cycle, usually one dominant follicle matures and releases its egg at ovulation; during gonadotropin stimulation, several follicles are driven to grow at once. Clinics track follicle number and size by ultrasound to judge how stimulation is progressing. The count of growing follicles is one of the key measures used to monitor treatment and its risks.

Plain EnglishA follicle is a small fluid-filled sac in the ovary, and each one holds a single egg. The sac's lining is made of support cells that respond to FSH, feeding the egg and helping it grow. In a natural cycle, usually only one follicle fully matures and releases its egg. During fertility treatment, the hormones push several follicles to grow at the same time — and doctors count and measure them on ultrasound. That count is one of the main ways clinics track whether the treatment is working safely.

The scienceThe corpus luteum — Latin for "yellow body" — is the temporary endocrine structure formed from the remnants of an ovarian follicle after ovulation. Its main role is to secrete progesterone, which stabilizes the uterine lining and prepares it for a possible pregnancy. It is maintained by LH activity in the days following ovulation; if no pregnancy occurs, it degenerates and progesterone falls, triggering menstruation. If pregnancy does occur, the hormone hCG from the early embryo takes over its support. In stimulated cycles, the corpus luteum's function is part of what clinicians consider when planning luteal support.

Plain EnglishAfter a follicle releases its egg, the leftover sac does not just disappear — it transforms into a temporary hormone factory called the corpus luteum, which means "yellow body" in Latin. Its main product is progesterone, the hormone that steadies the uterine lining and makes it ready for a pregnancy. LH keeps it running in the days after ovulation. If no pregnancy happens, it breaks down, progesterone drops, and the period starts. If pregnancy does happen, the early embryo sends its own signal — hCG — to keep the factory open.

The scienceThe live-birth rate is the proportion of treatment cycles, or of patients, that result in the birth of at least one living baby. It is the most patient-centered outcome measure in fertility medicine, because intermediate measures like egg count or fertilization rate do not guarantee a baby. When different stimulation drugs — such as urinary HMG versus recombinant FSH — are compared, live-birth rate per cycle started is the standard scoreboard. It is distinct from pregnancy rate, which counts pregnancies that may not all reach birth. Researchers and clinics report it so that treatments can be judged by the outcome that actually matters.

Plain EnglishThe live-birth rate measures how many treatment cycles end with an actual baby being born. It is the outcome patients care about most, because good numbers on eggs or embryos do not always lead to a birth. When doctors compare fertility drugs — like HMG versus recombinant FSH — this is the scoreboard they use. Note that it is different from the pregnancy rate, since not every pregnancy ends in a birth. It is the number that answers the only question that really matters: did this treatment work?
